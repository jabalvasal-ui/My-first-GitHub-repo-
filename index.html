2026 — My Tech Journey:

🚀 The Turning Point After Class 12

After completing my 12th (HSC) examination, I was confused about what to do next. There were many options: doing a course, taking a drop year to prepare for competitive examinations, taking admission to a BSc program, or choosing something completely different.

But I had always liked making and building projects. I also enjoyed drawing, giving speeches, doing science experiments, and playing chess. So, the most important question at that time was: What should I choose to build my career? 🎯

I started preparing for the MHT CET examination when only about 20–30 days were left. I joined PW's 20-day crash course , but after a few days, my Physics teacher told me that it was very difficult to do much in such a short time. At that point,only 13 days were left. ⏳

I already knew that other students had been preparing for one or even two years. But I kept going. Honestly, I became seriously focused when only five dayswere left!

But that was okay. I decided to give my best during those five days. Whatever the result would be, I didn't want to worry too much about it. I started believing that a college or university does not decide everything about a person's future.

I thought about people like Elon Musk, who became successful by focusing on what he wanted to build and achieve. This made me think: Should I also focus more on doing what I truly want to do rather than only doing what is considered necessary?

However, I also understood that this approach can be difficult for a student from a middle-class family. Financial stability is important, and I needed to find a practical solution that could support both my career and my dreams.

Then I came across a reel from one of Raj Shamani's podcasts. In that reel, he talked about first becoming financially independent enough to reduce family and social pressure. The idea was simple: earn enough to support yourself, reduce financial stress, and then use that stability to work towards your bigger dreams. 💰

That thought stayed with me.

After my second attempt, I started exploring my interests and trying many things that I had never done before. I started creating apps and websites using Google AI Studio. 🌐 I really enjoyed building websites that could solve particular problems.

But there was one major problem: I didn't know coding.

Whenever I asked AI to add something, remove something, or change something on a website, it sometimes created something completely different from what I had imagined. It was frustrating and difficult to control.

So, I decided to learn coding myself. 💻

I started learning the basics of coding and chose Python 🐍 as my first programming language. From that point onward, I started enjoying coding more and more. I liked solving problems using code and building things by myself.

That's when I decided that I wanted to enter the technology field.

When my MHT CET results were announced, I scored good .Both attempt results were almost the same, but I was satisfied that I had given my best.

After that came the CAP rounds and other admission processes.

In the first CAP round, I got CSE at a private autonomouse college.I liked the campus, faculty, and the project-based opportunitiesthere, so I decided to freeze my seat.

And finally, I took admission to B.Tech in Computer Science and Engineering (CSE). 🎓💻

That's how everything happened.

It wasn't a perfectly planned journey. There was confusion, uncertainty, last-minute preparation, failures, experiments, and many changes in my thinking. But every step helped me understand myself a little better. 🌱

And this is where the next chapter of my journey begins. 🚀
